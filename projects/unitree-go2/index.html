Project 01 · Robot Perception

Unitree Go2 BEV Navigation

Adapting BEVFormer-style perception from the nuScenes domain to short-range environment representation and navigation experiments on Unitree Go2.

2026.01 – 2026.03BEVFormernuScenesROS2PyTorchOpenCV
Status. Experimental research / learning project. Perception, mapping, and navigation-oriented components were developed and tested in stages, but a complete end-to-end autonomous navigation stack was not fully integrated.

Overview

The project began with a camera-based BEVFormer perception pipeline trained on the nuScenes dataset. I explored how a perception model designed for autonomous-driving scenarios could be adapted to a smaller quadruped robot with a different camera height, field of view, motion pattern, and navigation scale.

nuScenes→BEVFormer→Go2 Adaptation→Temporal Consistency→Drivable Area→Global Map→Nav2 Export

1. Close-range BEV Adaptation

The BEV region was reduced for short-range mobile-robot navigation while maintaining a 50 × 50 grid. This increased the spatial detail around the robot instead of allocating most cells to distant regions.

±51.2 mOriginal-scale reference · 2.048 m/cell
±25.6 mFirst adaptation · 1.024 m/cell
±10 mGo2 close range · 0.4 m/cell

Camera-only perception was used, with Go2-oriented camera-height augmentation and a compact BEV region intended for local navigation.

2. Go2 ROS2 Bag & Odometry Pipeline

I built a dataset path for sequential Go2 ROS2 bag data. Camera frames and odometry are synchronized to create frame pairs, while the robot motion between frames is represented as ego-frame translation and yaw change.

/camera/image_raw/odom→timestamp alignment→(frame t, frame t+k)+Δx, Δy, Δyaw

The pipeline also includes a Go2-specific camera model and conversion of odometry into the motion representation used by the BEVFormer temporal pipeline.

3. Self-supervised Temporal Consistency

Because robot-specific data did not have dense nuScenes-style labels, I explored a self-supervised adaptation strategy based on temporal consistency. BEV predictions from two nearby frames are aligned using odometry and compared after ego-motion compensation.

BEV(t)→ego-motion warp→KL consistency←BEV(t+k)

The loss design included valid/FOV masks, confidence filtering, reduced weighting for moving-object classes, entropy regularization, and teacher anchoring / knowledge distillation to reduce collapse during adaptation.

4. Temporal Voting

Single-frame BEV predictions could contain unstable or hallucinated obstacle regions. To suppress these, past occupancy predictions were warped into the current ego frame using odometry and combined over a temporal window.

BEV t−2BEV t−1BEV t→odometry alignment→majority voting→spatial filtering

5. Geometry-based IPM Experiment

I also explored a non-learning alternative for drivable-area detection using inverse perspective mapping (IPM). The same ground region observed from different robot poses should be photometrically consistent after odometry compensation if the flat-ground assumption is valid.

Camera→IPM→BEV→multi-frame warp→consistency voting

3D objects violate the ground-plane assumption and tend to become inconsistent across viewpoints, providing a geometric obstacle cue without a trained neural network.

6. Global Mapping & Nav2 Export

Local free-space estimates were transformed into a world frame using odometry and accumulated across frames. Cells were considered drivable only when repeated observations consistently supported free space.

Local BEV→Temporal Vote→World Transform→Global Accumulation→PGM + YAML

The final representation could be converted into free / occupied / unknown cells and exported in a Nav2-compatible PGM + YAML format.

What I Learned

  • How domain assumptions change when moving a perception model from autonomous cars to a small robot.
  • Practical handling of ROS2 bag images, odometry, camera geometry, and temporal alignment.
  • BEV warping and temporal consistency under ego motion.
  • The difference between learned semantic perception and geometry-based drivable-area estimation.
  • Why perception output alone is not yet a complete navigation system.
