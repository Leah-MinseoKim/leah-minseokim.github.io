Pipe Robot Localization

Landmark recognition and drift correction for autonomous localization in geometrically degenerate pipe and tunnel environments.

2026.04 – 2026.09ROS1LiDARGeometric DescriptorPlace RecognitionPose Graph Optimization
Research question. How can a robot recover longitudinal drift when locally similar pipe geometry provides almost no constraint along the direction of travel?

Overview

Long pipes and tunnels are highly repetitive along their longitudinal axis. Local LiDAR registration can remain consistent laterally while accumulating large position error along this weakly constrained direction. This project focuses on recognizing cross-sectional structural landmarks and using them as metric constraints for trajectory correction.

ROS1 Robot→LiDAR + IMU→Landmark Extraction→Descriptor Matching→Geometric Verification→Pose Graph

1. Degeneracy-aligned Landmark Extraction

Consecutive LiDAR scans are accumulated in a common local frame and the longitudinal corridor axis is estimated from the local point cloud. Structural boundaries such as pipe joints and rings are detected from axial surface variations and represented as cross-sectional landmarks orthogonal to the degenerate direction.

Local Scan Accumulation→Axis Estimation→Boundary Detection→Reference Contour

2. Geometric Descriptor

I worked on a degeneracy-aligned contour descriptor that unwraps the neighborhood around each cross-sectional boundary into a 2D chart. Each observed cell stores a signed normal offset from a fitted reference contour, preserving subtle surface variations that help distinguish landmarks with nearly identical cross-sectional profiles.

Boundary Neighborhood→Contour Coordinates→2D Unwrapping→Signed Offset Descriptor

3. Invariant Matching

Matching is designed to remain robust to reverse traversal, partial surface coverage, and small reference-contour errors. Candidate correspondences are evaluated under both relative headings, comparison is restricted to mutually observed cells, and low-order field distortions are removed before local patch matching.

0° / 180° HeadingValid-cell MaskingField Normalization→Local Patch Matching

4. Geometric Verification

Descriptor similarity alone can still produce false matches in repetitive tunnels. Mutual nearest-neighbor patch correspondences therefore undergo local geometric verification, and only a unique candidate with sufficient spatial consensus is accepted as a landmark association.

Patch Correspondences→Mutual Nearest Neighbors→Geometric Consensus→Verified Landmark Pair

5. Pose-graph Drift Correction

Verified landmark pairs provide geometric factors that constrain cross-section position and corridor-axis alignment. These factors are optimized together with the original relative odometry constraints under a robust loss, reducing accumulated longitudinal drift without imposing unsupported constraints around the common corridor axis.

Odometry FactorsLandmark Factors→Robust Pose Graph→Corrected Trajectory

6. Robot & Sensor Integration

The field inspection robot was operated with ROS1 and used LiDAR and IMU sensing in underground pipe experiments. LiDAR–inertial motion estimates are used for scan accumulation, while gravity provides a consistent transverse orientation for the descriptor.

Evaluation

The method is evaluated on the public GEODE benchmark and on field recordings from underground pipe inspections. The field dataset includes seven outbound/return sequences in polyethylene and concrete pipes with diameters from 400 to 800 mm.

What I Worked On

  • ROS1-based robot operation and LiDAR/IMU data handling for underground pipe experiments.
  • Automatic estimation of the geometrically weak corridor direction.
  • Cross-sectional landmark detection and contour-based geometric descriptor design.
  • Direction-invariant landmark matching with partial-observation handling.
  • Geometric verification to suppress false associations in repetitive environments.
  • Pose-graph constraints for correcting longitudinal drift from verified landmark matches.
