Project 02 · Ongoing Research

Pipe Robot SLAM

Localization under severe axial degeneracy in repetitive pipe environments using LiDAR, camera, IMU, structural joint observations, geometric verification, and factor-graph optimization.

2026 — PresentFAST-LIVO2VINSGTSAMEDCirclesLoFTRGICP
Research question. How can localization remain globally consistent when the pipe provides strong radial geometry but very weak and repetitive information along its axis?

Problem

Inside a long cylindrical pipe, many LiDAR scans look nearly identical after translation along the pipe axis. This creates a degeneracy direction: lateral position and orientation may remain well constrained while axial motion can drift or collapse. Repeated visual appearance also makes loop correspondence ambiguous.

LiDAR + Camera + IMU→FAST-LIVO2 / VINS→Axial Drift

1. Structural Joint Perception

Pipe joints provide sparse structural landmarks that are more informative along the degenerate direction. Video frames are processed with EDPF + EDCircles to detect circle / ellipse candidates. Frame-wise detections are linked over time rather than being accepted independently.

Video→EDPF→EDCircles→Tracklets→Joint Hypotheses

2. 3D Joint Hypotheses & Association

Joint observations are projected into 3D using the current trajectory. Multiple observations are grouped into candidate physical joints, and outbound / return traversals are associated using geometry, ordering, persistence, and appearance cues.

2D Joint Observation→3D Projection→Axis-density Clustering→Physical Joint IDs

3. Appearance Re-identification

Because neighboring joints can be visually similar, I explored annulus-based appearance descriptors. The circular neighborhood is unwrapped into a polar representation and divided around the circumference, where binary descriptors are compared with cyclic shifts and reverse-direction handling.

Joint Annulus→Polar Image→32 angular bins→BRIEF signature→Hamming matching

4. Loop Proposal & Geometric Verification

Visual correspondence can still be ambiguous in a repetitive pipe. A later pipeline therefore separates proposal from verification: image matches generate loop candidates, and raw LiDAR scans are aligned with GICP. Candidate transforms are then compared globally so that only geometrically consistent constraints enter the backend.

LoFTR proposals→GICP alignment→Transform consensus→Loop Factor

5. Factor-graph Backend

The frontend does not directly move the trajectory. Its purpose is to produce reliable structural constraints. GTSAM then combines these constraints with the original odometry / trajectory factors and solves for a globally more consistent trajectory.

Odometry FactorsJoint / Loop Factors→Robust Optimization→Corrected Trajectory

Research Experiments

Experiment 01

Joint-event Process-noise Injection

Used confirmed joint events as one-time triggers that increase pipe-axis velocity covariance in FAST-LIVO2 without directly applying a pose correction. The goal was to find how much additional uncertainty the existing estimator could tolerate before becoming unstable.

Experiment 02

3D Joint Density Clustering

Projected repeated image detections into 3D and studied density along the estimated pipe axis to recover candidate physical joint locations.

Experiment 03

BRIEF-based Joint Re-identification

Compared outbound and return observations using polar-annulus binary descriptors, cyclic shifts, and Hamming cost.

Experiment 04

Joint Landmark / Relative-factor Backend

Investigated ways to connect repeated observations of the same structural joint through shared or relative constraints in GTSAM.

Experiment 05

LoFTR + GICP Global Consensus

Generated multiple image loop proposals, verified each in 3D, grouped similar correction transforms, and selected a globally consistent factor set.

Evaluation Plan

The evaluation is designed to separate detector behavior from full-backend performance. Useful measurements include outbound/return joint residuals, trajectory change, repeated-scan point-cloud consistency, and voxel-level overlap at multiple resolutions.

Current Direction

The current emphasis is on making the frontend conservative: many visually plausible loop candidates exist inside repeated pipe geometry, so a good backend factor must be supported by both local geometric alignment and global correction consensus.
